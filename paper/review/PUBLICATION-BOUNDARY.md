# Publication boundary for manuscript-history review

The author requested preservation of recoverable manuscript history in the repository and an opportunity to inspect all recoverable differences before accepting changes. This bundle publishes evidence and a review tool, **not a newly approved paper or editorial decision set**.

Included: exact named manuscript/figure/bibliography artifacts, source aliases and hashes, historical Git paper snapshots, full PDF text extractions, the earlier human-provided lineage record, author-selectable proposals, and QA. Original bytes are retained; normal manuscript author names, affiliations, and contact information are part of those manuscripts.

Excluded: the three raw collaborator DOCX files and their comment text, private document extractions, raw model conversations, unrelated Downloads files, and author/QA decision exports. A QA-only export found during an exploratory recovery was excluded by filename from the final fresh recovery; it was never accepted as an author decision. Recovery now requires private extraction output to be outside the repository and separate from the public output tree.

The independent reviewer checked the intended original-file set, hashes, document exclusions, and obvious credential/transcript patterns. After the fresh recovery, the coordinator reran exact-byte tests and scanned included text/artifacts for common private-key, token, and quoted credential-assignment patterns; there were no matches. The only included JSON originals are figure provenance and the earlier manuscript-lineage manifest. These are scoped publication checks, not a guarantee that automated pattern matching detects every kind of sensitive information.

All 124 included original files match the manifest; three additional DOCX identities are metadata-only. No original manuscript, figure, numerical result, or frozen historical quotation is rewritten by this publication. Review exports remain in the author's browser/downloads and are not sent to a server. Public hosting, if added later, must preserve that local-only decision behavior.
