# Critique memo: integrated Molarium perspective draft

## Review method

These are four deliberately separate review passes performed from different professional viewpoints. They are not critiques from independently instantiated external models or human reviewers. The point of separating them is to prevent one editorial objective from silently dominating the others.

## Reviewer A — Scientific claims and epistemology

### Overall assessment

The revised argument is much stronger than a software-showcase paper because it makes the claim ladder explicit: execution, implementation parity, performance, and prediction require different evidence. Molarium is useful precisely because its current results stop at different rungs for different components.

### Major concerns

1. **Do not let one retrospective project carry an industry-wide claim.** Molarium can illustrate that bounded scientific infrastructure may be generated rapidly; it cannot estimate a universal productivity multiplier or prove that the entire CADD implementation layer is commoditized.
2. **Treat all current quantitative results as developmental.** The parity and speed numbers come largely from one primary browser/GPU stack. They require a frozen result ledger, fixture hashes, commit, browser, adapter, driver, and timing boundaries before becoming publication-grade claims.
3. **The three layers are not cleanly separable.** Protonation, charge assignment, precision, integrators, stopping rules, and display defaults can affect scientific conclusions. “Implementation” must mean reconstructible under an explicit contract, not scientifically neutral.
4. **The conformer result is correctly modest.** Added diversity is supported; better conformer prediction is not. This distinction should remain non-negotiable.
5. **“Existence proof” is too strong until provenance is released.** Use “documented case” and “source of falsifiable hypotheses.”

### Revisions applied

The draft now calls Molarium an illustrative case rather than a controlled productivity study, explicitly notes that active time is not reconstructed, labels numerical results provisional, defines porous layer boundaries, and adds a dedicated limitations section.

## Reviewer B — Molecular simulation and software systems

### Overall assessment

The manuscript accurately distinguishes the direct Sage engine, the STORMM-inspired homogeneous-replica engine, and the ANI-2x path. It also correctly avoids calling the first an OpenMM GPU platform or the second a source port of STORMM.

### Major concerns

1. **State the scientific scope in the main text.** The engines are nonperiodic, omit PME and several production features, and are not general replacements for OpenMM or native GPU software. ANI-2x and OpenFold have narrow domains. Arbitrary browser-native protein parameterization remains experimental.
2. **Baseline discipline is essential.** OpenMM Reference WebAssembly is a useful arithmetic oracle but not a modern native CPU or CUDA performance competitor. Replica timings omit setup and readback, and integrators differ.
3. **Security language must remain technical rather than absolute.** Local Lab provides an auditable browser-policy boundary, but a hosted page controls delivered code. A reviewed local checkout with verified hashes is the stronger trust model.
4. **Shared parameters are not an independent scientific oracle.** Agreement between two evaluators can preserve the same upstream parameterization error.
5. **The performance lesson should include the negative result.** WebGPU loses on tiny jobs and becomes useful when the workload is large or batched. That is a more credible systems result than a headline speedup.

### Revisions applied

The integrated draft now includes the current engine boundaries, privacy caveat, explicit benchmark qualifications, native-CUDA comparison, and the small-workload failure before the larger-workload speedups.

## Reviewer C — CADD industry and organizational strategy

### Overall assessment

The strategic thesis is compelling when framed as a shift in the marginal economics of implementation, not the disappearance of scientific-software companies. The three-layer framework is the strongest conceptual contribution.

### Major concerns

1. **Avoid a vendor strawman.** Mature applications contain edge cases, compatibility work, support, governance, and trust that are not reproduced by generating a screen or wrapper.
2. **Describe the moat shift as a hypothesis, not an accomplished market fact.** Better models, proprietary data, validated skills, secure deployment, and stewardship are plausible durable assets, but the commercial transition will be uneven.
3. **Make “skills” concrete and governable.** A scientific skill is not a prompt. It needs versions, domain limits, controls, uncertainty, provenance, acceptance tests, and an owner.
4. **Molarium is not itself proprietary discovery intelligence.** It demonstrates the enabling pattern: public methods and expert decisions becoming executable. The company-specific layer would require internal protocols, project history, and experimental feedback.
5. **Preserve the tool-space/model-space distinction, but keep it brief.** The paper should not become a separate manifesto about molecular foundation models.

### Revisions applied

The draft now acknowledges the accumulated value in mature applications, treats the industry implication as a possible shift rather than a proven transition, and presents Molarium as a precursor to governed organizational skills rather than as one already.

## Reviewer D — Perspective editor

### Overall assessment

The integrated structure is substantially clearer than either previous attempt:

1. concise thesis;
2. three layers of value;
3. Molarium as the case;
4. evidence separated into implementation, performance, and prediction;
5. what remains scarce;
6. organizational implications and limits.

### Major concerns

1. **The title remains intentionally vulnerable.** It will attract attention, but some venues may prefer “Molecular Design Software Is Becoming Generated Infrastructure.” The subtitle does important corrective work.
2. **Do not let the paper split into an opinion article followed by a software paper.** The transitions must continually explain why each Molarium detail tests the thesis.
3. **Keep only representative numbers in the main article.** A complete ledger belongs in supplementary material or the repository. Exact results should earn their space by illustrating a general point.
4. **The three-layer figure should be the conceptual centerpiece.** The build-loop figure is the strongest Molarium figure. The architecture figure is optional depending on venue and length.
5. **The abstract was too long.** It has been reduced and now identifies the case as illustrative.
6. **The ending should not introduce a new claim.** It now returns to the same implementation-versus-prediction distinction established at the beginning.

## Shared judgment

All four passes agree on the strongest version of the paper:

> Agents can now generate a meaningful amount of computational molecular-science infrastructure under expert direction. Molarium makes that claim concrete. The same case also shows why implementation parity, speed, and scientific prediction must be kept separate. As implementation becomes easier to reproduce, value shifts toward validated models, evidence, governed organizational skills, and scientific judgment.

They also agree on what the paper must not claim:

- a controlled productivity improvement;
- general scientific-software commoditization from one case;
- production-grade molecular simulation;
- cross-platform numerical equivalence;
- a general conformer-quality improvement; or
- prospective drug-discovery impact.

## Decisions still worth making with the authors

1. **Title:** retain the provocative “Molecular Design Software Is Now Free. Molecular Prediction Is Not.” or use the less vulnerable “Molecular Design Software Is Becoming Generated Infrastructure.”
2. **Article type and venue:** a 3,500–4,500-word Perspective can carry the thesis and case; a software journal will demand a larger technical and reproducibility package.
3. **Quantitative density:** keep representative parity and performance numbers in the main text, or move nearly all of them into a boxed case-study table.
4. **Latent-space discussion:** keep the current short bridge, or expand it only if Psiformer becomes a genuine second case rather than a speculative aside.
5. **Submission gate:** freeze the repository state, release the machine-readable result ledger and redacted provenance archive, and tie every quantitative statement to those artifacts.
