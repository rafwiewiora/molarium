 WORKING PREPRINT | 17 AUGUST 2026


 Replica-synthesized molecular dynamics in
 the browser
 Direct validation of a STORMM-inspired WebGPU engine in the Marco molecular modeler



 ABSTRACT
 We implemented an isolated-boundary molecular-mechanics engine in WebGPU for the Marco browser molecular modeler,
 adopting STORMM's strategy of advancing many independent systems in one GPU synthesis and fixed-point,
 order-independent accumulation. Direct comparisons used upstream STORMM commit 3eeccbde5192. Alanine dipeptide
 (22 atoms) and Trp-cage (304 atoms) passed preregistered energy and all-force thresholds for every supported vacuum
 term and OBC2 generalized Born. Sixteen-replica NVE tests, bitwise replay, replica isolation, and replica-local
 SHAKE/RATTLE constraints also passed. On one NVIDIA L4, Marco reached 0.51x and 0.73x upstream CUDA throughput
 in flexible vacuum and OBC2 simulations, and 0.39x and 0.52x with 12 X-H constraints at 2 fs. The study establishes
 numerical agreement and useful performance for a deliberately limited browser engine; it does not establish feature parity
 with STORMM.

 1 Motivation
 STORMM combines many molecular systems into a synthesis to fill a GPU efficiently and uses fixed-precision
 representations to make parallel accumulation reproducible. This architecture is attractive in a browser, where one GPU
 command stream can replace repeated JavaScript-to-device round trips and users benefit from local, installation-free
 simulation. The original STORMM paper showed that batching many small implicit-solvent simulations can outperform
 independent processes using NVIDIA MPS [1].

 The present question is narrower: can a browser WebGPU implementation reproduce upstream STORMM's energy
 surface and forces for the terms it claims to implement, while preserving independent replica behavior and useful
 small-system throughput?



 2 Implementation
 The engine evaluates harmonic bonds and angles, cosine periodic torsions, isolated no-cutoff Coulomb and 12-6
 Lennard-Jones interactions with explicit exceptions, and OBC2 generalized Born with an optional ACE surface term. It
 accepts numeric pair-distance constraints; Marco derives X-H targets from equilibrium bonds and selects a 2 fs time step.

 One WebGPU workgroup advances each replica. Deterministic Gauss-Seidel SHAKE follows drift, and RATTLE projects
 velocities after the second kick. Contributions use split signed 64-bit fixed-point arithmetic: 222 units per kcal/mol for
 energy, 218 units per kcal/mol/A for force, and 232 units per A for authoritative coordinates. Rounded coordinate and
 constraint increments return to that fixed-point representation. The same seed produces bitwise-identical trajectories.

 3 Validation design
 3.1 Static energies and forces
 Alanine dipeptide and Trp-cage coordinates and AMBER topologies came from upstream STORMM. Native references
 were extracted from committed potential regression tests and snapshot arrays. Bonds, angles, periodic torsions, Coulomb
 (ordinary plus 1-4), and Lennard-Jones (ordinary plus 1-4) were tested separately and as a supported-term total. OBC2
 used external dielectric 78.5 with the surface-area term disabled to match the upstream polar-only snapshot.

   Quantity                    Pass rule

   Energy                      absolute error <= 0.02 kcal/mol or relative error <= 5 x 10-4

   Force                       relative RMS <= 2 x 10-3 or absolute RMS <= 5 x 10-4 kcal/mol/A

   NVE drift                   maximum normalized windowed drift <= 0.5%

   Constraint distance         maximum relative residual <= 2 x 10-5

 Table 1. Preregistered acceptance criteria encoded in the test programs.

 Alanine's one CMAP term (-0.38861139 kcal/mol) is not implemented; it is recorded but excluded from the supported total.
 Trp-cage requires no omitted term.

 3.2 Dynamics and constraints
 Sixteen alanine replicas were propagated for 5,000 unconstrained NVE steps at 1 fs in vacuum and matched OBC2.
 Windowed energy drift was normalized by 3NkBT/2 at 300 K. Two identical four-replica Langevin runs tested bitwise
 replay; a one-replica run and replica zero of a 16-replica synthesis tested cross-replica isolation.

 Constraint tests used four independent stacks of eight water molecules, with 16 coupled O-H constraints per replica, for
 500 Langevin steps at 2 fs and 1 x 10-5 relative tolerance. Tests covered distance and velocity residuals, replay,
 one-versus-four replica isolation, and an intentional iteration-limit failure. A separate 500-step water test compared Marco
 SHAKE/RATTLE with OpenMM Reference/CCMA.

 3.3 Performance
 The matched workload contained 16 alanine replicas and 10,000 steps in vacuum or polar-only OBC2. Flexible runs used
 1 fs. Constrained runs used 12 X-H distances and 2 fs; upstream STORMM used rigid_geom on. Each median
 contains five repeats. Upstream dynamics.stormm.cuda and WebGPU/Vulkan ran sequentially on one licensed
 NVIDIA L4 (driver 595.71.05, CUDA 12.9). Native STORMM used its default single-precision valence and nonbonded
 settings.

 For this replicated workload, Marco reaches 51-73% of upstream CUDA throughput without constraints and 39-52% with
 constraints. The larger constrained gap is consistent with Marco's general serial-per-replica Gauss-Seidel solver versus
 specialized native constraint machinery; constraint parallelization is therefore a concrete optimization target.

 4.4 Auxiliary laptop result
 On an M1 Pro, Marco WebGPU reached 12,009.1 ns/day in vacuum and 8,518.1 ns/day in OBC2, versus 10,460.8 and
 4,231.5 ns/day for a native CPU executable configured explicitly for double precision. These values demonstrate local
 browser execution but are not used for the primary speed claim because devices and precision modes differ.

 5 Limitations and defensible claim
 The browser engine is nonperiodic and all-pairs. It supports general numeric pair-distance constraints and X-H
 SHAKE/RATTLE, but not specialized SETTLE or parallel constraint-graph kernels. It lacks PME, periodic boundaries,
 neighbor lists, CMAP, GBn/GBn2, virtual sites, heterogeneous topologies within one synthesis, and free-energy methods.
 Marco currently caps a replica at 512 atoms. Production OBC2 uses external dielectric 78.3 and ACE; the polar-only 78.5
 configuration exists solely for exact upstream matching.


         For the listed isolated-boundary terms and two pinned AMBER fixtures, the Marco WebGPU
         engine reproduces upstream STORMM regression energies and forces within stated
         tolerances, preserves deterministic independent replicas with or without X-H constraints,
         and reaches 39-73% of upstream STORMM CUDA throughput for the tested 16-replica
         NVIDIA L4 workloads.


 It is not yet defensible to call this a full STORMM port or to extrapolate throughput to large periodic biomolecular
 simulations. The original paper's ff14SB side-chain-only, GBn, hydrogen-mass-repartitioned 4 fs workload is intentionally
 not treated as a direct comparator.



 6 Reproducibility
 Inputs, pinned source commit, source and executable hashes, complete force vectors, encoded thresholds, repeat
 timings, hardware labels, and result hashes are stored under stormm-study. Accepted same-L4 manifests are in
 results/l4-vws. The upstream source required one recorded compile-only member-access patch that changes no
 molecular-mechanics equation. The L4 runner pins the NVIDIA Vulkan ICD, rejects software adapters, runs native CUDA
 and WebGPU sequentially, records the environment, and hashes all outputs.
